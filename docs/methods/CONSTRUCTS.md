# Constructs

[Repository](../../README.md) / [Documentation](../README.md)

## Practical force

Practical force means that a control could affect the relevant action, state, decision, or consequence under the event's actual conditions. Capability, attempted action, downstream propagation, and effect are distinct propositions. A visible attempt can coexist with an unknown effect.

## Observability

Observability is the ability to resolve a specified proposition from the frozen public packet using a fixed coding rule. It is conditional on retrieval effort, access, evidence cutoff, and scope. It does not measure the probability that the control existed.

## The observation unit

An event-control observation binds one event, one control objective, one opportunity, and one relevant period. Multiple controls within an event share context and sources; analytical uncertainty must account for that clustering. A proposed control opportunity does not imply an implemented control.

## Evidence sufficiency

Evidence sufficiency is proposition-specific. Directness, traceability, specificity, temporal relevance, independence, consistency, and completeness remain separate dimensions. No additive trust or evidence score is defined.

## Practical human authority

Formal authority concerns assigned permission. Practical authority also depends on information, comprehension opportunity, available time, technical and institutional permission, viable alternatives, and a route to affect execution. The pilot assesses these separately. Conjunctive downstream theories require their own validation.

## Control functions

Control functions may overlap: preventive, detective, restrictive/corrective, recovery, authorization/governance, and human oversight. Annotators select applicable functions and justify the mechanism. An always-on restriction need not trigger like an alarm. A recovery control need not prevent the initial harm.
